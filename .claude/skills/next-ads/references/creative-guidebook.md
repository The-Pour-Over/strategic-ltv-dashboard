# The Pour Over — Creative Guidebook (psych concepts + felt needs)

The two controlled vocabularies behind every TPO ad. The `psych#` and
`feltneed` slots of the naming convention must come from these lists verbatim —
the dashboard's pattern read aggregates on them, so an off-list value is
invisible to the data.

## The 17 psychological concepts

| # | Concept | How it works in an ad |
|---|---------|----------------------|
| 1 | Reciprocity | Give value first to create psychological debt |
| 2 | Commitment & Consistency | Micro-yesses before the CTA |
| 3 | Social Proof | Testimonials, specific numbers ("Join 1.5M others") |
| 4 | Liking | Vulnerability, humanness, "we are the same" bond |
| 5 | Authority | Scale, proprietary data, expert positioning |
| 6 | Scarcity & Urgency | Deadlines, limited availability |
| 7 | Unity | Shared identity/tribe, badge of who they want to be |
| 8 | Headline Dominance | Clarity + intrigue to stop the scroll |
| 9 | Selective Filtering | Identity call-outs to grab a niche audience |
| 10 | Zeigarnik Effect (P.S.) | Open loops, cliffhangers, unfinished tasks |
| 11 | Pratfall Effect (Damaging Admissions) | Admitting flaws builds trust |
| 12 | Empirical Proof | Specific numbers over vague adjectives |
| 13 | Un-copyable Proof | Unique, un-googleable perspectives |
| 14 | "Reason Why" Bias | Always give a "because…" reason |
| 15 | Status Signaling | Frame as signal of success/intelligence/belonging |
| 16 | Singular Path | One clear CTA, no choice overload |
| 17 | Cognitive Fluency | Simple language, short sentences, 3rd-grade level |

## The 16 felt needs (emotions)

Loneliness · Stagnation · Deprivation · Worry · Guilt · Helplessness ·
Suffocation · Chaos · Lethargy · Insignificance · Desire · Waste · Isolation ·
Inadequacy · Anxiety · Resentment

(Lowercased single words in ad names: `anxiety`, `chaos`, `resentment`, …)

## Voice notes

- Warm, plain, Christ-first. Concept #17 applies to everything: short
  sentences, 3rd-grade reading level, no churchy jargon.
- A brief should EXECUTE its concept, not describe it — a #11 hook admits a
  real flaw ("I used to skip the news entirely…"), it doesn't say "we're honest."
